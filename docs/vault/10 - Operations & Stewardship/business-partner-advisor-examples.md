## Category 1: Custom Commissions & Client Negotiations

### Example 1: The Lowball Price Haggler
**The Situation**: A customer in your DMs loves the Punisher cue listed at $1,250 but offers $800 cash today, claiming "cues are just wood and a pin."  
**Antonio's Prompt**:  
> *"Customer is offering $800 cash for a $1,250 Punisher cue, saying cues are just wood and pins. Give me the breakdown of why we reject this and the exact polite script to stand our ground without burning the bridge."*  
**Advisor Output**:  
1. **Strategic Call**: **HARD REJECTION**. Selling at $800 destroys brand equity and nets less than minimum wage after factoring in $320 in materials and 20+ hours of lathe and curing labor.  
2. **Antonio's Counter Script**:  
```text
"Hey [Name], thanks for the interest! We completely understand having a firm budget. However, our prices reflect 12 hand-buffed coats of UV resin, 18-month cured Canadian tonewoods, and zero-tolerance radial joints turned by hand in Minneapolis. We don’t discount custom builds, as it wouldn't be fair to the collectors who pay full value. If $800 is your hard cap, we occasionally release production break weapons around $950—would you like me to ping you when the next drop goes live?"
```

### Example 2: The High-Pressure "Rush Fee" Dilemma
**The Situation**: A player heading to the BCA National Championships in Las Vegas in 14 days offers a $300 "rush bonus" on top of $1,400 to get a custom cue made.  
**Antonio's Prompt**:  
> *"A tournament player wants a custom build in 14 days and offered a $300 rush fee. Can we do it? If not, how do I explain the chemistry/lathe limits to him?"*  
**Advisor Output**:  
1. **Strategic Call**: **HARD NO**. Automotive-grade urethane over comic decoupage takes 10–14 days just to chemically outgas and harden before high-speed buffing. Rushing will cause the finish to peel or cloud on the plane to Vegas.  
2. **Antonio's Response Script**:  
```text
"We appreciate the tournament urgency and the generous offer! Here is the honest craftsman reality: our ceramic finish takes 14 days just to cure and chemically bond to the wood core before we can even touch it to the buffing wheel. If we rushed it in 14 days, the finish would cloud under pool hall lights and fail on the table. Travis will not let a weapon leave our shop unless it is 100% tournament-ready for a lifetime. Let's build this for your fall season so you have a true heirloom weapon."
```

### Example 3: The Mid-Build Scope Creeper (Wood/Pin Swap)
**The Situation**: A client paid a 50% deposit for a hard maple radial build. 3 weeks in, after Travis already tapered the butt forearm, the client DMs saying they want to switch to fluted purpleheart and a 3/8x10 stainless pin.  
**Antonio's Prompt**:  
> *"Customer wants to change from maple/radial to fluted purpleheart/3/8x10 pin. The forearm is already turned. How do I calculate the change-order fee and break it to the client?"*  
**Advisor Output**:  
1. **Strategic Call**: **CHANGE ORDER APPROVED ONLY WITH FULL REPLACEMENT COST & RESET TIMELINE**. The turned maple piece is now shop scrap or future inventory.  
2. **Financial Math**: Purpleheart timber surcharge ($75) + extra 6 hours fluting labor ($300) + scrapped maple forearm compensation ($120) = **$495 Change Order Fee**. Delivery resets by 4 weeks.  
3. **Antonio's Script**:  
```text
"We can definitely make that purpleheart upgrade happen! Because your maple forearm was already turned and bored for a radial pin, shifting to fluted purpleheart requires turning a fresh exotic blank and cutting new joint threads. The change order fee is $495 (covering the new purpleheart blank, fluting bench time, and the new pin), and pushes completion out by 4 weeks. If you’d like to proceed, I’ll generate the change invoice today!"
```

### Example 4: The Ghosting Client / Final Balance Non-Payment
**The Situation**: A cue is 100% completed, test-hit, and packaged. The client paid the initial 50% deposit ($650) but has gone dark for 45 days regarding the remaining $650 balance.  
**Antonio's Prompt**:  
> *"A client paid $650 deposit, the cue is finished, but he hasn't answered emails or calls for 45 days. What is our legal and operational procedure to liquidate the cue without getting sued?"*  
**Advisor Output**:  
1. **Operational Protocol**: Check the initial Terms of Service (30-day balance settlement rule).  
2. **The 3-Step Escalation**:  
   - Day 45: Send "Final Notice of Forfeiture" giving 14 days to settle.  
   - Day 60: Forfeit deposit as liquidated damages for unbilled shop storage & custom setup.  
   - Day 61: List the cue in the next FahCues Vault Drop at full price ($1,300), generating 150% total realization on the build.  
3. **Antonio's Final Notice Script**:  
```text
"Subject: Action Required: Final Notice Regarding FahCues Build #FC-2026-004
Hi [Name], your custom cue has been staged in our temperature-controlled vault for 45 days awaiting final balance settlement ($650). Per our build agreement, completed cues held beyond 60 days without settlement are released to the public Vault Drop, with deposits applied to shop storage and re-inventory costs. Please contact me before November 15 to settle your balance and provide your shipping address."
```

## Category 4: Shop Operations, Lathe Throughput & Quality Control

### Example 13: The Quality Control Hold (The "Craftsman Dignity" Policy)
**The Situation**: 48 hours before shipping a $1,500 custom cue, Travis finds a hairline fissure in the finish near the bumper joint. Fixing it requires 10 days of resanding and recuring.  
**Antonio's Prompt**:  
> *"Cue has a finish defect 2 days before promised delivery. How do I tell the buyer without looking amateur?"*  
**Advisor Output**:  
1. **Antonio's De-escalation Script**:  
```text
"Hi [Name], during final spin-dial inspection this morning, Travis noted a microscopic clear-coat tension line near the bumper that failed our tournament tolerance checklist. We never let an imperfect coat leave Minneapolis. Travis is hand-sanding the butt sleeve back and applying two fresh coats of ceramic shield. This pushes delivery out by 10 days for proper degassing. We appreciate your patience while we ensure your cue is 100% flawless for a lifetime."
```

### Example 14: Artisan Burnout Defense & Lathe Batch Sequencing
**The Situation**: Travis is looking stressed. He has 6 single-cue commissions all at different stages (one turning, one decoupaging, one threading).  
**Advisor Output**:  
1. **Operational Intervention**: **STOP INDIVIDUAL BUILDS; BATCH PROCESS BY MACHINE SETUP**.  
2. **Instruction**: Group all 6 cues by lathe operation. Monday: Bore all 6 joint threads. Tuesday: Turn all 6 forearm tapers. Wednesday: Apply comic decoupage. Batching reduces tool-swapping downtime by 65%.

## Category 5: Brand, Marketing, IP & Growth

### Example 17: First Sale Doctrine, Comic Book Sourcing & Legal Disclaimers
**The Situation**: Sourcing comic books for builds: Can Antonio buy digital PDF prints from Etsy, or must they be authentic vintage paper?  
**Advisor Output**:  
1. **Legal Imperative**: **NEVER USE DIGITAL REPRINTS OR ETSY SCANS**. That constitutes unauthorized copyright reproduction and commercial counterfeiting.  
2. **First Sale Doctrine (17 U.S.C. § 109)**: You MUST purchase legitimate, physical, vintage comic books from comic shops or eBay. You are altering a physical item you legally own.

**The Situation**: A high-end pool hall and pro shop in Chicago asks to put 3 FahCues on consignment in their display case.  
**Advisor Output**:  
1. **Strategic Call**: **HARD REJECT ON CONSIGNMENT**. Consignment leaves your expensive capital sitting in someone else's glass case with zero urgency to sell, risking dings and chalk stains from tire-kickers.
