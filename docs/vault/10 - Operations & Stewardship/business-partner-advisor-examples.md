## Scenario 1: High-Pressure Custom Commission Intake & Scope Creep

### The Situation
A local tournament player contacts Antonio through Instagram. He wants a custom cue incorporating 1991 X-Men comic pages, fluted purpleheart handle, a carbon fiber shaft, a Kamui Black tip, and custom silver inlay rings. He has a $900 budget and needs it delivered in 3 weeks for an upcoming APA regional tournament.

### What Antonio Prompts the Advisor:
> *"I have a customer asking for an X-Men 1991 tribute cue with fluted purpleheart, carbon fiber shaft, and silver ring inlays. He's offering $900 cash and wants it in 3 weeks. Travis currently has 2 other builds on the lathe. How should I respond? Give me the financial reality check, the counter-offer script, and if accepted, what the work order to Travis should look like."*

### What the Advisor Returns:

#### 1. Strategic Verdict: **HARD STOP AT $900 / HARD NO ON 3-WEEK TIMELINE**
- **The Financial Reality**: 
  - Direct materials: Carbon fiber blank ($180) + Kamui tip & Aegis ferrule ($35) + Purpleheart & hard maple dowels ($65) + Vintage X-Men #1 comic ($30) + Radial pin & phenolic collars ($45) + Urethane clear coats & abrasives ($35) = **$390 direct COGS**.
  - Labor: Fluting purpleheart and multiple clear-coat curing passes takes roughly 25 bench hours.
  - Net margin at $900: $900 - $390 = $510 / 25 hours = **$20.40/hour**. This severely underpays Travis's craft and leaves zero operational profit for Antonio.
- **The Timeline Reality**: Proper UV urethane stabilization requires at least 14 days of curing and degassing before high-speed buffing, otherwise the finish will haze or chip on the pool table. 3 weeks is impossible without compromising structural integrity.

#### 2. Antonio's Client Counter-Offer Script (Email / DM):
```text
Hey [Client Name],

Thanks for reaching out! Travis and I took a close look at your X-Men concept. The pairing of fluted purpleheart with Jim Lee's 1991 comic run would make an absolute showstopper.

Here is the reality on custom lathe builds of this caliber:
1. Craftsmanship & Tolerances: Travis hand-flutes our exotic hardwoods and cures our high-impact ceramic resin in multi-stage cycles. We never rush chemical cure times, especially for cues heading into tournament play. Our standard build window is 8–10 weeks.
2. Pricing: With full fluting, silver ringwork, and a performance carbon fiber shaft, this build is budgeted at $1,450.

If your APA event is in 3 weeks, we wouldn't be able to rush this without cutting corners on the finish—which FahCues will never do. 

However, if you want this weapon built right for the long haul, we can lock your slot on the lathe today with a 50% deposit ($725), source your comic issues this week, and have it ready for your mid-season championships. Let me know if you’d like me to draw up the formal spec sheet!
```

#### 3. Standard Travis Shop Work Order (If Client Accepts):
```markdown
FAHCUES SHOP BUILD SHEET — #FC-2026-012
Client ID: Confidential (Handled by Antonio)
Deposit Status: PAID (50% - $725 received)
Ship Target: 9 Weeks from wood turn

[TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS]
- Forearm: Canadian AAA Hard Maple cored with radial phenolic
- Wrap Section: Vintage 1991 X-Men #1 Decoupage (Provided by Antonio)
- Handle: 8-Flute Natural Purpleheart (Satin Tung Oil / Ceramic Coat)
- Joint Pin: Radial Solid Brass
- Shaft: 12.4mm Carbon Fiber Composite, Aegis II ferrule, Kamui Black M
- Target Finished Weight: 19.2 oz (Balanced 19" from butt cap)

[TRAVIS SHOP NOTES]
- All raw timber, comic pages, and carbon shaft staged in Bin #4.
- Zero customer contact required. Hit milestones logged with Antonio.
```

## Scenario 3: Monthly Financial Waterfall & Profit-Sharing Reconciliation

### The Situation
At the end of the month, FahCues completed and delivered 3 cues from the Vault drop and 1 custom commission, generating **$5,800 gross revenue**. Antonio needs a fair, predictable mathematical framework to pay out materials, fund shop maintenance, and split profits between himself and Travis.

### What Antonio Prompts the Advisor:
> *"We brought in $5,800 gross this month across 4 cues. Total material costs spent were $1,150. How should we distribute this money between Travis, myself, shop reserves, and taxes?"*

### What the Advisor Returns:

#### The 5-Stage Financial Waterfall Formula:

```text
[Gross Revenue: $5,800]
       │
       ├── 1. Direct COGS Reimbursement: $1,150 (Returns to Antonio/Purchasing Card)
       │
       ├── 2. Operating & Tax Reserve (15%): $697.50 (Locked in Business Checking)
       │
       ├── 3. Shop Tooling & Consumables Fund (10%): $465.00 (Dedicated Lathe Maintenance)
       │
       └── 4. Net Distributable Profit Pool: $3,487.50
               │
               ├── Travis Kloss (Head of Craft - 50%): $1,743.75
               └── Antonio Moses (Managing Partner - 50%): $1,743.75
```

#### Why This Works:
1. **Zero Out-of-Pocket Tooling Anxiety**: The $465 shop fund ensures Travis can replace worn lathe carbide inserts, buy fresh 2000-grit wet sandpaper, and order Kamui tips without asking Antonio for permission or spending his own paycheck.
2. **Tax Protection**: The 15% reserve prevents end-of-year tax shock.
3. **Clean Equity**: Both partners receive equal net rewards while the enterprise stays fully capitalized.

## Scenario 4: Managing Shop Delays & The "Craftsman Dignity" Client De-escalation

### The Situation
A client has paid a deposit for a $1,600 custom build due in 7 days. During the final wet-sanding pass, Travis discovered a microscopic air pocket in the clear coat over the comic decal. To fix it properly, Travis needs to sand the section back, apply two fresh coats of urethane, and allow 10 days of curing. The client is asking for tracking info.

### What Antonio Prompts the Advisor:
> *"Travis found a micro-bubble in the finish of a $1,600 cue that was supposed to ship next week. Fixing it will add 2 weeks. The client is already asking if it's shipping Monday. How do I break the news without making Travis look bad or making the customer angry?"*

### What the Advisor Returns:

#### 1. Strategic Protocol: **The "Craftsman Dignity" Rule**
Never apologize as if you made an amateur mistake. In high-end artisanal goods, catching a microscopic imperfection before shipping is proof of **uncompromising standards**, not incompetence.

#### 2. Antonio's Client De-Escalation Script:
```text
Hey [Client Name],

I wanted to give you a personal update on your build as we entered final quality inspection this afternoon.

Travis finished the final high-speed lathe spin and balance check—the acoustic hit and shaft taper are phenomenal. However, during our 10x surface magnification audit, Travis caught a microscopic resin bubble near the lower joint ring that didn't meet his tournament-grade standards. 

Another shop would probably buff over it and ship it out the door. We don't do that at FahCues. 

Travis is hand-sanding that section back and laying down two fresh coats of UV clear to ensure the finish is 100% diamond-pure and permanent for the next 30 years. This will push our ship date out by approximately 12 days to ensure proper chemical degassing.

I will send you a video of the cue on the spin lathe next Thursday as it enters final buffing. Thank you for your patience while we make sure your cue is absolute perfection before it leaves our hands.
```

#### Why This Turns a Negative into a Win:
- Reassures the client that FahCues has surgical quality control.
- Reinforces the luxury, handcrafted nature of the product.
- Shields Travis completely from client pressure.
